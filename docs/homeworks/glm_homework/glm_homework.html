Homework 15 — GLM & Logistic Regression

Generalized linear models

Generalized linear models for count data: fitting a linear model, a Poisson GLM and a negative binomial GLM to the same social-spider colony data, and finding out that all three give a different answer.
Author

Bill Perry

📥 Social spider colony data 📥 Homework script (.R)

Note📚 Where these data come from

Source: Whitlock, M.C. & Schluter, D., The Analysis of Biological Data, Ch. 17, practice problem 15.

Social spiders live in communal webs. For 17 colonies, two things were recorded: the height of the colony above the ground (cm) and the number of spiders living in it.

The question: does colony size change with height above the ground?

In class you analysed the Galápagos islands — a count where the Poisson failed and the negative binomial fixed it — and the New England ants, a count where the Poisson was already right. This is a third count, and it behaves differently from both.

Warning⚠️ Read this before you start

This dataset will give you three different answers depending on which model you fit — and one of them is “there is no relationship.” Your job is not to pick the one you like. It is to work out which model you were entitled to, and to say so.

Do not skip Part 2. The wrong model is part of the assignment.

Assignment Overview

You will fit three models to one question and compare what they claim. The statistical content is the dispersion check and what it does to a standard error.

Learning Objectives

  1. Recognise a count response and say why a linear model is the wrong tool for it
  2. Fit a Poisson GLM and test it for overdispersion
  3. Fit a negative binomial GLM and confirm the overdispersion is gone
  4. Say what overdispersion does to standard errors and p-values, and in which direction
  5. Check a GLM with DHARMa rather than with a test that does not apply
  6. Run a sensitivity check on an influential observation without deleting it
  7. Report a count model properly, and build a figure on the right scale

Data Description

17 colonies, three columns.

Key variables:

  • colony — colony ID, 1–17 (not a predictor)
  • height_cm — height of the colony above the ground, cm — the predictor
  • n_spiders — number of spiders in the colony — the response, a count
